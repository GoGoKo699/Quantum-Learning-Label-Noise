# The supervised learning model

The task is to learn a hidden Pauli measurement from labeled quantum specimens. With correct annotations, a learner can identify the measurement using a linear number of specimens and polynomial time. Random annotation errors preserve the linear collective sample complexity at every fixed noise rate below one half. Under a quantum hardness assumption for ordinary classical learning parity with noise, however, a fixed positive error rate prevents every polynomial-time quantum learner from obtaining a nonnegligible prediction advantage.

The feature source, target class, and clean test task are the same throughout. The noise acts on the recorded training labels. Quantum processing is ideal. This chapter fixes the definitions and statements used by the other science chapters; the proofs of the individual results are linked below.

## 1. Pauli conventions and the target prior

There are $`n\geq 1`$ feature qubits. Write

```math
d=2^n,\qquad D=2d,\qquad M=2(d^2-1).
```

For $`a,b\in\mathbb F_2^n`$, define

```math
\nu(a,b)=\sum_{j=1}^n a_jb_j\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
W_{(a,b)}=i^{\nu(a,b)}X^aZ^b.
```

The exponent $`\nu(a,b)`$ is an **integer sum**, before reduction modulo four in the power of $`i`$. Thus $`W_{(a,b)}`$ is the canonical tensor product of $`I,X,Y,Z`$, with no additional sign. For example, $`iXZ=Y`$. Every $`W_p`$ is Hermitian, squares to the identity, and satisfies

```math
\operatorname{Tr}(W_pW_{p'})=d\,\mathbf 1\{p=p'\}.
```

For $`p=(a,b)`$ and $`z=(a',b')`$, the bit-valued quantities used in Bell sampling are

```math
q(p)=\sum_j a_jb_j\pmod 2,
\qquad
[p,z]=a\cdot b'+b\cdot a'\pmod 2.
```

In particular, $`W_p^T=(-1)^{q(p)}W_p`$ and conjugation by $`W_z`$ multiplies $`W_p`$ by $`(-1)^{[p,z]}`$.

The hidden target is

```math
P=\sigma W_p,
\qquad
p\in\mathbb F_2^{2n}\setminus\{0\},
\qquad
\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}.
```

Unless another prior is explicitly stated, $`p`$ and $`\sigma`$ are independent and uniform. The resulting prior is uniform over the $`M`$ signed nonidentity Pauli operators. Both signs are part of the target set: changing $`P`$ to $`-P`$ exchanges the two class labels. In particular, the target prior has exact sign symmetry.

## 2. Clean specimens and corrupted annotations

A clean example is generated by drawing a balanced class sign $`Y\in\{+1,-1\}`$ and supplying one feature specimen with conditional density operator

```math
\rho^0_{Y,P}=\frac{I+YP}{d}.
```

This is the normalized projector onto the $`Y`$ eigenspace of $`P`$. The two classes have orthogonal supports. If $`P`$ is known, measuring $`P`$ predicts $`Y`$ without error. This measurement can be implemented by measuring the nonidentity single-qubit Pauli factors and multiplying their outcomes.

For a training example, the source records

```math
C=YE,
\qquad
\Pr(E=-1)=\eta,
\qquad
\gamma=1-2\eta,
\qquad
0\leq\eta<\frac12.
```

The flip variable $`E`$ is independent of the clean label and specimen, and the flips are independent across examples. We use uppercase letters for signs. Their lowercase counterparts are bits: $`Y=(-1)^y`$, $`C=(-1)^c`$, and $`E=(-1)^e`$.

The recorded label is balanced. Conditioning on it gives

```math
\rho_{C,P,\gamma}
=\frac{I+C\gamma P}{d}.
```

It is useful to write an entire annotated example as a classical–quantum state:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{P,\gamma}
&=\frac12\sum_{c=0}^1
|c\rangle\langle c|\otimes
\frac{I+(-1)^c\gamma P}{d}\\
&=\frac{I_D+\gamma Z_C\otimes P}{D}.
\end{aligned}
```

Here $`Z_C`$ acts on a register representing the **classical** annotation. This notation does not supply a coherent superposition of labels or a purification of the source. Conditioned on the hidden target, $`T`$ training examples have state $`\tau_{P,\gamma}^{\otimes T}`$.

A fresh test specimen is drawn from the clean source $`\rho^0_{Y,P}`$. Its true label is withheld. The learner outputs a sign $`\widehat Y`$. Consequently, the test problem remains realizable with zero Bayes error at every training noise rate.

The density-operator description is compatible with a source that prepares a pure product state on every realized draw. The preparation variables are inaccessible to the learner; averaging them gives precisely the states above. An explicit preparation is given in [Source preparation](source_preparation.md). Since only one independently drawn specimen is supplied per example, operational probabilities depend on these density operators and their tensor products, regardless of the inaccessible ensemble decomposition.

## 3. Access and resource accounting

The learner receives the specimens and their recorded classical labels. It receives no description of a latent pure state, hidden eigenbasis, preparation circuit, or target Pauli. There is no coherent example oracle, QRAM, or instruction to prepare additional copies of a particular latent specimen. Pairing two examples means using two independent draws from the source.

The unrestricted statistical model permits arbitrary joint quantum processing of the training examples. A trained model may contain quantum memory and classical information, and its use on the single fresh test specimen may consume it completely. The learner need not identify $`P`$, output a classical hypothesis, or return a member of the Pauli hypothesis class.

The efficient model requires the entire training and prediction procedure to run in quantum polynomial time, using a polynomial number of specimens. A circuit that is only known to exist at the statistical level does not meet this requirement. The computational assumption in [Computational hardness](computational_hardness.md) uses the same uniform quantum polynomial-time convention as the learner.

An **individual-example learner** measures each supplied training specimen separately, with no quantum memory persisting from one specimen to the next. Its choice of POVM can depend on the complete previous classical transcript. It may use unlimited classical memory and computation, fresh ancillary systems within each measurement, and arbitrary classical randomization. After training it may choose any measurement on the fresh test specimen based on that transcript. This restriction is on coherent access between specimens, rather than on computational effort or on the complexity of an individual POVM.

Quantum gate errors are not part of this model. The parameter $`\eta`$ describes annotation errors alone. The positive algorithms can use the noise rate, or a supplied lower bound on $`\gamma`$ sufficient to choose their sample sizes; the hardness result remains valid when the noise rate is known.

## 4. Prediction and identification are different guarantees

For a learner $`A`$ using $`T`$ training examples, define

```math
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{clean}}(A;n,\eta,T)
&=\Pr(\widehat Y=Y),\\
a(A;n,\eta,T)
&=p_{\mathrm{clean}}(A;n,\eta,T)-\frac12.
\end{aligned}
```

The probability averages over the signed-uniform target prior, all independent source draws, the clean test label, and the learner's randomness and measurement outcomes. Let $`a_T^*`$ denote the supremum of this average advantage over unrestricted quantum learners. Ignoring the training set and guessing gives advantage zero, so $`0\leq a_T^*\leq 1/2`$.

A fixed positive prediction advantage means a constant $`a\in(0,1/2)`$ independent of $`n`$. The lower bounds below are average-case statements for the stated target prior. They therefore also rule out a guarantee of that advantage uniformly over all targets; they do not assert that every individual target is hard.

Identification is a stronger, separately stated objective:

```math
\Pr(\widehat P=P)\geq 1-\delta.
```

An identification algorithm followed by measurement of $`\widehat P`$ has clean prediction success at least $`1-\delta`$, since it succeeds perfectly whenever identification was correct. Hence it has advantage at least $`1/2-\delta`$. No converse reduction from a good predictor to an explicit Pauli description is assumed. In particular, the computational lower bound applies directly to prediction, including a consumable quantum model.

### A single-test identity

For any fixed target and any trained quantum model, averaging over training outcomes induces a binary POVM on the fresh feature register. Write its observable as $`A_P`$, where

```math
-I\leq A_P\leq I.
```

Then its clean advantage for that target is

```math
a_P=\frac{1}{2d}\operatorname{Tr}(A_PP).
```

If the same prediction is compared with an independently corrupted held-out annotation, its agreement probability is

```math
p_{\mathrm{corrupt},P}
=\frac12+\gamma a_P.
```

Both identities hold for arbitrary quantum memory and a decoder that consumes it.

**Proof.** Let $`\omega_P`$ be the trained state, including its classical transcript, and let $`F_+,F_-`$ be the final two-outcome POVM on the model and test register. The effective test observable can be written as

```math
A_P=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{model}}
\left[
(\omega_P^{1/2}\otimes I)
(F_+-F_-)
(\omega_P^{1/2}\otimes I)
\right].
```

It is Hermitian and lies between $`-I`$ and $`I`$ because the effective test effects form a POVM. The balanced clean source gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(\widehat Y=Y\mid P)
&=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}
\left[\frac{I+A_P}{2}\rho^0_{+,P}\right]
+\frac12\operatorname{Tr}
\left[\frac{I-A_P}{2}\rho^0_{-,P}\right]\\
&=\frac12+\frac{1}{2d}\operatorname{Tr}(A_PP).
\end{aligned}
```

Independence of the held-out label flip gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(\widehat Y=C\mid P)
&=(1-\eta)\Pr(\widehat Y=Y\mid P)
+\eta\Pr(\widehat Y\ne Y\mid P)\\
&=\frac12+\gamma a_P.
\end{aligned}
```

This calculation uses a single execution of the decoder. It makes no reuse or hypothesis-extraction assumption. Averaging over $`P`$ proves the corresponding average-case identities. Equivalently, the corrupted-label risk and clean risk obey $`R_\eta=\eta+\gamma R_0`$. ∎

## 5. Canonical result index

### K1 — Efficient learning with correct annotations

At $`\eta=0`$, for any $`\delta\in(0,1)`$, there is a learner that identifies every signed nonidentity target with probability at least $`1-\delta`$ using

```math
m=2n+1+\left\lceil\log_2\frac1\delta\right\rceil,
\qquad
T=2m+1
```

training specimens. It Bell-measures $`m`$ disjoint pairs, solves an affine system over $`\mathbb F_2`$, and uses one additional specimen to determine the global sign. Its total training time is polynomial in $`n`$ and $`\log(1/\delta)`$. Its output is a classical description of the target Pauli, and prediction uses single-qubit Pauli measurements.

Bell sampling and a separate sign-learning step are established Pauli-learning mechanisms; see [HKP2021](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#hkp2021) and the broader quantum-memory framework in [CCHL2021](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#cchl2021). The complete argument with the present label and phase conventions is in [Bell learning](bell_learning.md#k1--efficient-clean-learning).

### B1 — Exact affine noisy-parity data from Bell pairs

For two independently drawn training examples, Bell-measure their feature registers and keep the product of the recorded labels. With the Bell convention in [Bell learning](bell_learning.md#b1--the-exact-bell-correspondence), the resulting classical observation obeys

```math
Z\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb F_2^{2n}),
\qquad
R=(-1)^{q(p)+[p,Z]}F,
\qquad
\Pr(F=-1)=\eta_B=2\eta(1-\eta).
```

The noise $`F`$ is independent of $`Z`$, and independent pairs yield independent observations. Its contrast is $`1-2\eta_B=\gamma^2`$. The signed target's global sign cancels in this observation and is determined separately.

This is an exact transformation of the supplied data into classical affine learning-parity-with-noise data. It is not, by itself, a lower bound on every quantum learner. That lower bound requires H1.

### S1 — Sharp collective sample complexity for prediction

For $`0<\gamma\leq1`$ and $`T\geq1`$, every unrestricted quantum learner satisfies

```math
a_T^*\leq
\min\left\{
\frac12,
\frac12\sqrt{
\frac{(1+\gamma^2)^T-(1-\gamma^2)^T}{M}
}
\right\}.
```

With no training examples, $`a_0^*=0`$. Conversely, there is a collective measurement that identifies the target with error at most $`\delta`$ whenever

```math
T\geq
\left\lceil
\frac{4}{\gamma^2}\ln\frac{M-1}{\delta}
\right\rceil.
```

Thus the collective sample complexity of any fixed positive clean prediction advantage is $`\Theta(n/\gamma^2)`$. The lower bound concerns prediction itself; the upper bound is obtained through the stronger objective of identification. The identification measurement may require exponential computational effort. The finite-$`T`$ displayed inequality is a trace-norm bound derived from an exact Hilbert–Schmidt identity, not a closed-form evaluation of the optimal advantage.

See [Collective statistics](collective_statistics.md) for the full proof, including the pairwise fidelities and the construction of a measurement compatible with classical labels.

### S2 — Exponentially many specimens for individual-example learning

Every individual-example learner obtaining average clean advantage $`a`$ from $`T`$ noisy training examples satisfies

```math
a\leq
\min\left\{
\frac12,
\frac{5(T\gamma^2+1)}{d+1}
\right\}.
```

In particular,

```math
T\geq
\frac{a(d+1)/5-1}{\gamma^2}
```

whenever the numerator is positive. For fixed positive advantage and sufficiently large $`n`$, this is $`\Omega(2^n/\gamma^2)`$. Unlimited classical computation and adaptive choice of individual-example POVMs are included. The additive one in the bound accounts for the single clean test specimen.

The proof adapts the learning-tree method for Pauli ensembles, including its sign symmetry, to heterogeneous contrasts. See [Individual measurements](individual_measurements.md) and the antecedent [CCHL2021](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#cchl2021).

### H1 — Conditional computational hardness at fixed positive noise

Fix a constant $`\eta\in(0,1/2)`$. Assume that ordinary classical decision-LPN at noise rate $`\eta`$ is hard for uniform quantum polynomial-time algorithms receiving polynomially many classical samples. Then every uniform quantum polynomial-time learner in this model has negligible average clean prediction advantage, even with polynomially many specimens, arbitrary coherent training, and an arbitrary consumable quantum model.

A learner with average advantage $`a`$ gives a quantum decision-LPN distinguisher with ideal distinguishing advantage $`\gamma|a|`$, using LPN dimension $`n-1`$ and one additional held-out example. A strict bounded-runtime implementation of uniform Clifford sampling preserves this gap up to negligible loss. This is a direct reduction from prediction, without identifying the learned Pauli or extracting a reusable classical hypothesis.

See [Computational hardness](computational_hardness.md) for the assumption, exact simulation, runtime accounting, and proof. No computational hardness is claimed unconditionally, or for every noise sequence that tends to zero.

## 6. What the resource comparison establishes

| Access and processing | Training specimens for fixed clean advantage | Computational guarantee |
|---|---:|---|
| Coherent training, correct annotations | $`O(n)`$ | Polynomial time by K1 |
| Unrestricted coherent training, contrast $`\gamma>0`$ | $`\Theta(n/\gamma^2)`$ | Statistical existence by S1 |
| Bell pairs with exhaustive classical decoding | $`O(n/\gamma^4)`$ | Exponential decoding time in the explicit algorithm |
| Adaptive individual-example measurements | $`\Omega(2^n/\gamma^2)`$ | Bound allows unlimited classical computation |
| Polynomial-time coherent training at a fixed hard LPN noise rate | Polynomially many specimens do not give nonnegligible advantage | Conditional on H1's quantum decision-LPN assumption |

At fixed positive noise below one half, S1 and S2 retain an exponential separation in the number of specimens, while H1 prevents efficient training under the stated assumption. The exhaustive Bell decoder supplies an elementary example of statistical learnability with expensive classical processing; it need not attain the optimal dependence on $`\gamma`$.

For noise that decreases with dimension, the clean-batch algorithm in [Bell learning](bell_learning.md#vanishing-noise-a-clean-batch-algorithm) has polynomial cost when $`\eta=O(\log n/n)`$. This positive regime and the fixed-rate conditional lower bound leave a range of scalings unclassified. At $`\eta=1/2`$, which is excluded from the main statements, each training example is $`I_D/D`$ and contains no information about $`P`$.

The relationship to previous quantum-memory separations, noisy stabilizer learning, and coherent noisy-example models is documented in [Related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md). The physical and computational interpretation is developed in [Interpretation](interpretation.md).
